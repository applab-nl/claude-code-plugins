# Styles

37 looks. Pick by "Good for" first, then read only the chosen `styles/<id>/card.md`.

## Diagrams with depth

- **Blueprint Build** (`blueprint-build`, explainer, needs strokefont.js): A real drafting sheet: deep blueprint blue with grain and worn fold creases, a title block, a construction grid, crisp white linework in two weights with hidden edges dashed, dimension arrows and numbered notes in plotted single-stroke capitals. The drawing turns out to be a 3D wireframe, and then a solid object standing on its own plan.
  Good for: Plans, set-ups and how-it-is-made: first the layout as a flat drawing, then the same thing as an object, with dimensions and two or three numbered pointers attached to the places they talk about.
- **Data Skyline** (`data-skyline`, explainer): A bar chart built as an architect's model: towers of stacked pale stone blocks on a printed plinth, with one tower in vermilion, tiny trees and people for scale, and thin drawn annotations on top.
  Good for: Any list of labelled values: a comparison, a ranking or a series over time. Each value is a tower, the label is printed at its foot, the unit is stacked in countable blocks and one accent colour marks the bar that matters.
- **Exploded View** (`exploded-view`, explainer): A product teardown film: one object hangs over a pale seamless floor, comes apart along its axis into its parts, and every part gets a numbered callout in ink blue.
  Good for: How something is built or what is inside it: any object made of parts. Each part is one named item, the order of the row is the order of assembly, and putting it back together shows where everything goes.
- **Layer Stack** (`layer-stack`, explainer): A system shown as a stack of thin glass sheets, each with its own diagram printed in white, floating one above the other over a dark machined plinth. Aqua edges glow like edge-lit acrylic against near-black; one amber is kept for the answer.
  Good for: Anything built in layers: a tech stack, a product teardown, how a request travels through a system. One sheet per layer with a simple diagram, a name and a short line beside it, and a pulse that shows the order things happen in.
- **Node Network** (`node-network`, explainer): A physical model of a network mounted on a painted wall: glazed porcelain tokens in brass rings, joined by thin brass rods, like a museum's molecular model. Each token is two-sided and turns over to its coloured enamel face when the news reaches it.
  Good for: Anything that is things and the connections between them: spread, referrals, teams, systems, flows. Nodes sit in oval rings around a centre, sized so the finished model fills a 16:9 frame; links are rods, a pulse travelling along a link is the story, and a number plaque per ring carries the count.
- **White Model** (`white-model`, explainer): An architect's presentation model: everything is matte white card and foam on a white site board, and one saturated accent colour is reserved for the thing being explained.
  Good for: How something flows through a system: energy, water, money, data, people. Build the system as a white model, lay the path through it as conduits, and let the accent travel the path in the right order while labels name each stop.
## Filters over 3D

- **Dithered** (`dithered`, explainer): A lit 3D scene shown in one-bit: two colours only, big square pixels, every shade made of ordered dot patterns, one-pixel contours and bitmap lettering in desktop windows, like an early home computer screen with real depth behind it.
  Good for: Any object or small set-up staged like a still life, with desktop windows carrying the numbers: a readout for the value that changes and a strip for sequence or progress. Good for routines, cycles, steps, states and anything with a clock in it.
- **Ink and Toon** (`ink-toon`, explainer): Real 3D that reads as a hand-inked animation cel: flat colour in two tones, a brush line round every shape, halftone dots where light turns to shadow and pen hatching inside cast shadows, printed on warm paper inside a ruled panel.
  Good for: Steps, recipes and how-tos with a few recognisable objects: each step is one bold action on the same hero object, named by a big lettered word and a hand-written caption box.
- **Low-Poly** (`low-poly`, explainer): A miniature world of flat, faceted shapes, like folded paper or cut stone: every triangle is one tone, and neighbours differ slightly, inside a short palette of sand, coral and cream against teal water and sage greens.
  Good for: Processes that happen in a place: cycles, journeys, supply chains, how a system of parts feeds itself. Each stage is a spot in the landscape with a paper flag, and the camera travels the route between them.
- **Painted** (`painted`, story): A filter, not a set: any lit 3D scene comes out as an oil painting on canvas, with thick directional brush dabs, broken colour, ridges of paint that catch a raking light, linen weave showing through the thin darks and a ragged unpainted edge. Here it carries a golden-hour countryside: a wheat field, a windmill, a bakery cottage.
  Good for: Stories, journeys and processes that happen in a place, told as a picture book: how something is made, where something comes from, a day in a life, a chain of cause and effect. Each beat is one stop in the landscape where one thing visibly happens and sets off the next; something that travels (wind, a road, a river, a character) leads the camera from stop to stop; one short caption per beat sits in the bare corner of the canvas and names what is happening. The filter carries any 3D scene, so the world can be a farm, a city, a workshop or a room, as long as each subject is a big simple shape.
- **Pencil Sketch** (`pencil-sketch`, explainer, needs strokefont.js): A real 3D scene that reads as a graphite drawing in a designer's sketchbook: forms shaded with hatching and cross-hatching that follows the light, outlines that overshoot their corners, faint construction lines, hand lettering, toothy paper with a smudge and an eraser mark, and one red accent.
  Good for: How something is built or put together, step by step: parts are drawn in order, each with a numbered note. Also good for anatomy of an object, plans, exploded views and anything that benefits from seeing the structure through the surface.
- **Voxel** (`voxel`, explainer): Everything is built from small cubes, like a hand-made voxel model on a display base: a short palette with every cube a shade apart from its neighbours, tiny bevels that catch the light, and the label spelled in cubes on the front of the base.
  Good for: Anything that can be shown as a small model that builds up in stages: how something grows, is made or is put together. One base, one subject in the middle, one voxel word per stage, weather or helpers as small cube events around it.
- **X-Ray** (`x-ray`, explainer): A radiograph you can move around: the object turns into glowing film on a dark scanner bed, every wall brightest where you look through the most material, with one warm accent for the part that matters right now.
  Good for: What is inside something and how it works: anatomy, machines, products, buildings. Up to three inner parts, each lit and named in turn, with a simple flow (heat, air, current, force) shown acting on them.
## Flat content made physical

- **Embossed** (`embossed`, title): Words and one small mark pressed into thick cotton paper and filmed in macro: grey-blue letterpress sunk deep enough to see the walls of the impression and the shadow inside it, a ruler scale embossed blind (raised, no ink), and a copper foil stamp, on a deckle-edged sheet lying on a dark press bed.
  Good for: Any quote, name, number or simple logo: it is drawn flat on a canvas and pressed into the material, so the statement arrives as a finished, tactile object. Best for one line that should feel permanent, with one small mark picked out in foil.
- **LED Wall** (`led-wall`, story): A giant outdoor screen made of real lamps: thousands of frosted round LEDs in black louvred cabinets on a steel gantry at night. From far away it is a crisp bright picture; close up it falls apart into single glowing lamps, each with a red, a green and a blue chip under its lens.
  Good for: Any 2D animation, diagram or story that can be drawn at lamp resolution: a fable with two characters, a process with parts that move along a track, a chart that grows, a map with a route, a game-like sequence. Draw it on a small canvas with one pixel per lamp. A beat is something happening in that picture (a character sets off, stops, is overtaken, arrives); a label is one short dot-font line in the ticker strip; what moves is the pixel picture, while the wall, its housing and the light it throws stay the same and take their colour from whatever is playing.
- **Neon Sign** (`neon-sign`, explainer, needs strokefont.js): A line drawing bent out of real glass tube and mounted on a painted brick wall at night: bright cores, coloured gas, black-painted returns into rubber boots, clear standoffs, every unlit tube waiting as milky glass. Small tube lettering for captions. Wet ground at the foot of the wall mirrors the end of it.
  Good for: How something works, as a diagram that switches on in the order it happens: light, electricity, signals, water, traffic, money, a recipe, a journey. A beat is a group of tubes that strikes on because the one before it did. Outlines are the things, a chase of short segments is whatever flows between them, alternating poses are whatever repeats (rain, flames, wheels, a heartbeat), and colour carries meaning, so use it for the part of the idea that really is about colour, state or category. One small tube caption per beat names what just lit; the last thing to light is the result, and it holds.
- **Particle Swarm** (`particle-swarm`, explainer): Sixty thousand tiny glass beads hanging in front of a matte paper wall. They pile into plump, rounded models of things (a seed, a drop, a cell, an organ, a heap) that fill most of the frame and throw a soft shadow on the wall, change colour and strain while they hold, then let go and fly on to the next formation.
  Good for: Processes where stuff flows, gathers, splits, grows, heats up or bursts, and things that are made of many units (cells, molecules, people, grains, data points). The beads are the stuff itself: a beat is either a new formation the swarm flows into or a change that runs through a formation while it holds (it warms from one side, something inside it comes loose, it swells). Populations keep their colour and identity from start to finish, so the viewer can follow where each kind of thing ends up. One short caption per beat names what is happening; the beads never form words or numbers.
- **Pin Wall** (`pin-wall`, explainer): A pin-art toy the size of a wall, laid out like a relief model: tens of thousands of steel pins with rounded heads in a tight honeycomb, held in a dark bolted frame. The pin heights form a landscape you could put your hand on, and groups of pins carry a tint for what they stand for (here blue for sea, warm for rock, glowing orange for lava).
  Good for: Anything that can be shown as a moving relief or a moving greyscale picture: terrain, waves, growth, charts that rise, shapes that morph into each other. The content is one height function, relief(pin, time), which says how far each pin stands out and which tint it carries. A beat is a change in the relief that follows from the one before (calm, something rises, it breaks through, the result). Words are one short caption per beat on a small plate at the bottom; the pins never form words.
- **Shadow Box** (`shadow-box`, explainer): A paper-cut light box: opaque paper silhouettes stand one behind another in a deep walnut frame. The back of the box glows warm and bright, every sheet nearer to you is darker, and the nearest ones are dusk-blue shapes with a thin warm line of light on their cut edges.
  Good for: Sequences with a natural order in depth: timelines, eras, layers of a system, before and after. Each sheet is one stage, a big silhouette with a cut-out label, and the light shows which stage we are at.
- **Split-Flap Board** (`split-flap`, title): A mechanical departure board: rows of black flaps with white lettering in a dark steel frame, lit like a station hall at night, with one amber row for the point that matters.
  Good for: Lists, rankings and numbers: each row is one fact, revealed top to bottom, with the last or most important row picked out in colour.
## Machines that explain

- **Domino Chain** (`domino-chain`, explainer): A row of hand-made dominoes on a polished walnut table: bone faces riveted to ebony backs, each engraved and paint-filled with one step of the story, the last one in red. When a domino falls its label lies down the right way up, so the fallen row reads like a chart.
  Good for: Cause and effect, chain reactions, step-by-step plans and anything that compounds: every domino is one step, printed with a word or a number, and the fall carries the story from the first to the last. Sizes can grow, the row can bend and branch.
- **Factory Line** (`factory-line`, explainer): A toy-grade production line on a clean studio floor: chunky cobalt machine housings with big white labels, safety-orange moving parts, a dark rubber belt and one white page riding an orange tray.
  Good for: Any step-by-step process: the raw input rides a belt, each labelled station is one step and visibly changes the item, and the finished result is presented at the end.
- **Marble Run** (`marble-run`, explainer): A handmade wooden marble run shot like a product film: a walnut board carrying pale maple rails on brass pins, brass paddle gates, swirled glass marbles and three glass jars on a maple plinth with the labels burnt into the wood, in front of a shaded seamless backdrop.
  Good for: Sorting, routing and filtering: anything where many items take different paths through the same rules. The hopper is what comes in, each gate is one rule, each jar is one outcome with a counter under it, and how full the jars end up is the result.
## Material worlds

- **Glass and Light** (`glass-light`, explainer): Real glassware on a dark stone table in front of a glowing backdrop: thin clear jars and bottles turned on a lathe, holding coloured liquids with a visible surface, a meniscus and sharp boundaries between layers. Every liquid throws a pool of its own coloured light, with bright caustic lines, on the table in front of it. Small white measure marks are printed on the glass.
  Good for: Recipes, mixtures, ratios, amounts, chemistry: anything that fills, pours, layers or changes colour, told with real glassware. A beat is one action on the vessel (a pour, an addition, a shake, a wait). Amounts are levels against the marks on the glass, ingredients are colours, and a change of state is a change in the liquid (layers, cloudiness, colour). Labels are the measure marks plus one short caption line per beat.
- **Inflated** (`inflated`, explainer, needs strokefont.js): Everything is a glossy inflatable in a bright one-colour studio: here a striped hot-air balloon, its basket, a flame, arrows, clouds and small tube letters. Each skin has welded seams and is limp, flat and creased when short of air, round and mirror-smooth when full.
  Good for: Any explainer or story that can be told with a few simple inflated objects and characters. It is strongest where pressure, growth, floating, squeezing or too much and too little is the point, because how full each thing is carries the meaning. A beat is one change of air (something fills, lifts, vents, sags) that causes the next one; small balloon arrows show a push or a flow; each beat gets one short caption in small tube letters at the side of the frame.
- **Paper Pop-Up** (`paper-pop-up`, story): A pop-up book open on a wooden table in lamplight. Every scene is built from flat die-cut card: printed in a few flat colours, creased down the middle, standing in layers on real fold mechanisms, with a pale core showing on every cut edge.
  Good for: A story or a process in a few steps: one spread per step, each with one big subject in the middle, supporting pieces at the sides and one short line of words on a paper ribbon. A pull tab can show a change on the spot (fill, reveal, move).
- **Soft Clay** (`soft-clay`, explainer, needs text3d.js): Everything is modelled in plasticine and filmed like stop-motion on a small tabletop: chunky, slightly lopsided forms with fingerprints, tool marks and pits in them, in a few colours from one box of clay, in front of a seamless paper backdrop.
  Good for: Anything that can be told with a few friendly objects on a table: amounts as piles and stacks, growth and comparison, simple characters and props, a number or a few words as fat clay letters. Good for warm, non-technical topics.
- **Toy Blocks** (`toy-blocks`, explainer): Design-shop wooden blocks on a sunlit playroom floor: maple cubes and bricks with rounded edges, chalky milk paint worn back to bare wood at the corners, and letters, numbers and simple pictures screen-printed on the faces. The stacks are the chart.
  Good for: Lists, comparisons, proportions, steps and "the building blocks of X". One cube is one unit, a tower is a bar, a brick is a label, alphabet blocks spell the headline; pictures on the faces say what each unit is.
## Products and screens

- **Device Hero** (`device-hero`, product): A phone filmed like a launch video: a titanium body with a polished frame standing on black gloss in a dark studio, its screen the brightest thing in the room. Then the screen fills the frame and the interface becomes white cards floating over the glass.
  Good for: An app, a feature or any on-screen moment: show the device first so the viewer knows what they are looking at, then go inside and let two or three interface elements act out the one thing the feature does.
- **Floating Screens** (`floating-screens`, explainer): Screens as objects: white cards printed with a real interface, each backed by a block of clear glass, hanging one in front of the other in a bright, warm room. The newest card is always nearest and sharpest; the ones before it stay behind it, softly out of focus.
  Good for: Anything made of steps or screens: an app flow, a workflow, a process, a set of stats or quotes. One card per step, the action on each card acted out, a cable from each step to the next.
- **Hero Icon** (`hero-icon`, title): One big, chunky app-icon tile floats in a bright seamless studio of a single colour. A raised symbol built from thick rounded pieces sits on its face, and the whole thing looks like a toy you could pick up.
  Good for: A tool, product or idea as one object. Each thing it does (or each option, or each step) becomes one symbol on the tile with its own colour; the last face is the brand mark or the answer, with a short line of raised lettering on the wall beside it.
- **Studio Product** (`studio-product`, product): One physical product in a near-black seamless studio, filmed like a flagship launch: the object is carved out of the dark by strip softboxes and stands on a black mirror floor, with a single restrained line of type beside it.
  Good for: One product and one claim. The macro passes show what it is made of, the moving light and the counter act out the claim (here: a full day passes, it is still cold), and the lock-up states it in one line.
## Spaces and journeys

- **Circuit City** (`circuit-city`, explainer): A real circuit board filmed as a city at night: matte black chips are the buildings, aluminium capacitors the towers, gold tracks the streets, and white silkscreen print names each district. Deep green solder mask, gold and tinned metal, warm signal light.
  Good for: Journeys and pipelines: something travels through a system and each stage does its part. Every stage is a landmark with a printed label; the pulse and the camera carry the order.
- **Monument Type** (`monument-type`, title, needs text3d.js): Words as architecture: a short phrase stands as colossal letters on the terraces of a dark concrete monument in a flooded salt flat at sunset. One word is weathered steel and keeps the last sun; tiny people, lamps, birds and a crane give the scale.
  Good for: A short statement of two to five words where one word is the point: a principle, a motto, a verdict, a title. The hero word gets the top terrace, its own material and the last light; a word can be marked unfinished to put it under scaffolding.
- **Museum Gallery** (`museum-gallery`, explainer): A dark gallery hall at night: gilt-framed posters on a deep green damask wall, each in its own tight pool of warm spotlight with the beam hanging in the air, a brass plaque beside every frame, a rope barrier and a polished stone floor that mirrors it all.
  Good for: Lists and timelines of three to five points: each point is one framed poster with a plaque (a year or number, plus a few words), visited in order, with the last one as the conclusion.
- **Spotlight Stage** (`spotlight-stage`, story): A toy theatre at night: crimson velvet, a worn timber stage and a painted world of flat card cut-outs standing in layers, with an easel card at the front for the caption. The room is dark; whatever the story is about is picked out by shafts of light cutting through haze.
  Good for: Stories, history, fables and step-by-step scenes. One scene per beat: each beat is a stage picture built from flat cut-out scenery and cut-out actors, and the change between beats is shown, not cut. The follow spot says where to look, the colour of the wash says where and when we are, and one short line on the easel card names the scene (it can carry a running number).
- **Tiny Diorama** (`tiny-diorama`, explainer): A whole little world on one floating tile, like a designer's model on a desk: chunky rounded buildings, a toy van, peg people and lollipop trees in a few matte paint colours, on a thick slab of ground whose cut sides show soil, clay and rock.
  Good for: Places, systems and journeys: anything with a few stops and something that travels between them (a supply chain, a commute, how a request moves through a company). Each stop is a little building with a sign; the traveller links them in order.
